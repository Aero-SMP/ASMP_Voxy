Cache-loading diagnostics 265: measured results
Written 2026-10-06. Diagnostics only; no throughput algorithm was changed.

Result
The measured throughput gate in this warm-cache load is turnover of the 14
section-worker leases. Source work remained backlogged while completed jobs
continued holding those leases. CACHE and EMPTY completed cohorts spent 59.79%
of their combined reservation time after worker execution finished. This is
measured resource retention, not proof that one CPU thread ran continuously at
100%, or a controlled prediction of the speedup from any proposed fix.

The clearest specific capacity sink is empty-result publication: tasks computed
in about 13 microseconds but retained their slots about 54 milliseconds. The
CACHE task cohort spent 51.61% of its lease residence after completion. Meshing
was the largest useful worker service cost; file reading was much smaller.

Why empty results retain a compute slot
ClientSession.java:899 builds BuiltSection.emptyWithChildren for EmptyWorkerTask.
At ClientSession.java:3391, early admission release requires ref.bytes() > 0 and
rendererAdmitted(). A zero-geometry publication retains its lease until the
terminal upload outcome/activation; optional deferred saving can also delay
actual reuse. Source and measured EMPTY residence identify a concrete sink.
Releasing empty results earlier is a prospective ownership change, not part of
this diagnostics patch, and would need lifecycle/cancellation/fence-safe tests.

Owner work and useful worker service
Overall owner CPU during this same sustained window was 74.86% of one logical
core, 67.24% over the whole fill. This does not establish 100% CPU saturation.
DETAIL_REFINE consumed 2.7288 s elapsed and PUBLICATIONS 1.7060 s. There were 608,916
refinement actions, 608,874 reporting success, and 9,686,191 publication polls,
9,652,622 still pending. Success means demand children were ensured, not that
608,874 new meshes were built. These are concrete repeated-work targets.

Source: ClientSession.drainDetailMailbox at 1613, addChildren at 2020 and
pollPublications at 3379. Repeated refinement can reapply child demand/priority;
publication polling revisits admitted entries waiting for terminal outcomes.
These phase timings do not attribute every postcompletion delay to those loops.

Existing handoff means, with different cohorts, were mesh->claim 0.53978 ms,
claim->submit 0.63615 ms, submit->admission 0.07183 ms, admission->observe 0.36136 ms,
observe->reuse 0.000879 ms. They are explanatory, not an additive lease partition.
The latter admission stages exclude zero-geometry early admission releases.

Exclusive CACHE service across workers: MESH 32.1087 worker-seconds, about 0.96553 ms
per mesh; CACHE_DECODE 4.24943 s; NAME_RESOLUTION_WAIT 3.20818 s over 547 segments;

Measurement limits
Windows CPU accounting here is quantized AND deferred. Sustained WAIT reported
2.578125 s CPU over 1.7744 s elapsed, physically impossible for that single-thread
phase. Analyzer marks COARSE_DEFERRED_OS_ACCOUNTING_UNRELIABLE. Raw phase CPU
is retained but must not rank exact CPU consumers. Prefer long overall thread
CPU spans and monotonic paired lease partitions. Missing CPU is not zero.

Debug summary construction allocated 1.08-8.22 MB/capture and took 6.06-82.75 ms
during startup; sustained 1.09-1.12 MB/capture and 9.35-28.97 ms. This is an
instrumented run with overhead, not a calibrated comparison to 264. GC elapsed
MXBean counters are not stop-the-world pause measurements. Conditional worker
classifications at failed dispatches are point samples, not utilization time.
Fixed histograms, counters and matrices retain no per-section timing history.
Normal artifacts exclude debug telemetry classes, diagnostic clocks and fixtures.
